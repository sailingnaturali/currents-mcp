The long sound on north Graham Island joining Masset Inlet to Dixon Entrance.
The gate is Cook Point, not the sound — the streams elsewhere follow the trend of
the channel and are described as presenting no great difficulty, so the practical
plan is to carry the fair current through the length of the sound and meet the
constriction near slack. That plan is seamanship rather than a CHS instruction:
the published directions here are a pilotage sequence of bearings and transits
with no timing advice at all. Length is worth stating carefully — CHS's own
point-to-point distances sum to roughly 18 miles for the sound proper, and the
24-mile figure only holds measured from the outer bar through to Masset Inlet.

True slack is a few minutes, as Sailing Directions says of the Masset wharf, but
a usable window is wider than that reads: in the 2026 predictions the current
holds under one knot for a median of about 45 minutes and under half a knot for
about 30. The 5.8-knot peak is a top-of-year spring event — the median peak
through 2026 is 3.8 knots either way. Chart 3895's tidal information is flagged
by CHS as approximate; use the Masset tide station, not Wiah Point, when working
entrance depths. Note also that the current station and the tide-table entry
carry different names for the same gate: CHS's current file says Masset Channel,
while Sailing Directions and the tide tables say Masset Sound.
