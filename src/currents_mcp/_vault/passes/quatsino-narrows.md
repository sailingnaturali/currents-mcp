A mile and a half of narrows east of Drake Island, joining Quatsino Sound to
Holberg and Rupert Inlets on northwest Vancouver Island. The transit outlasts the
slack: 1.5 miles at 5 to 6 knots is 15 to 18 minutes, and on a big tide the
current stays under 1.5 knots for only about 10 minutes, then builds at roughly 2
knots per quarter hour. So this gate is entered on the turn rather than transited
inside a window, and the second half of the run is made against a stream that has
already started. On neaps the problem disappears — a weak day can hold under 1.5
knots for hours. Entering just before the turn and riding the new stream through
is standard practice, not CHS instruction; Sailing Directions describes the
streams and leaves the tactic to the mariner.

Two timing complications are published. The surface can turn at a different time
than the water beneath it, and on small tides the turn can run as much as an hour
late. The Pacific Pilotage Authority gates ships here harder than most places on
the coast: a vessel drawing up to 6.1 m may transit on any slack in daylight,
while one drawing up to 8.8 m gets high-water slack in daylight only, with
maximum length 185.9 m and beam 27.4 m. Deep-sea traffic through the sound has
thinned since the Port Alice marine berth was decommissioned in 2023, and the
Island Copper mine on Rupert Inlet closed in 1996, but the log booms and the
Coal Harbour water-taxi and seaplane traffic remain.
