      tide height. It runs seaward through most of the sea tide's rise and
      landward through most of its fall, so slack falls roughly 3.5 to 4 hours

      than applying an offset. Strong winds break the pattern outright. The
      entrance pinches to 0.2 mile between Carey Point and Noir Point, and a bar
      of irregular depths running northeast from Carey Point squeezes the
      navigable channel to 0.1 mile. Pilotage is compulsory for commercial
      traffic, so expect piloted ships in that width. Inside, the fairway is too
      deep for secure anchoring and the long reaches let swell in; Kellys Cove,
      opposite the Big Bras d'Or wharf, anchors in about 9 m out of the main
      stream.

The sea entrance to the Bras d'Or Lakes, on the northeast side of Cape Breton
Island. The rapid is at the outer narrows between Carey Point and Noir Point, not
at the Seal Island Bridge nearly five miles inside. Normal rates in the entrance
are 4 to 5 knots, rising to 6 when the lake is standing high; CHS's 2026
predictions peak at 4.6 knots, with the ebb holding a slight edge over the flood.
Sailing Directions publishes no slack duration and no recommended window here —
it refers the mariner to the current tables — so the 30 minutes carried above is
a planning figure, not a published one. The phase, not the window, is what
catches people out.

The inversion is a basin effect, and the tide ranges show why: 0.9 to 1.5 m at
the northeast entrances, 0.5 m inside, and 0.2 m at Baddeck on springs. The lakes
fill and drain slowly through a restricted channel, so the flow follows the head
difference between sea and lake rather than sea level itself, and the head
reverses about a quarter of a cycle late. Weather moves lake level 0.3 m or more
independently of the tide, which is why wind can advance, delay or reverse the
stream. Overfalls, tide rips and standing waves are not described here by CHS —
those accounts are secondary. Boats unwilling to take the gate enter the lakes
from the south through the St. Peters Canal lock instead, limiting depth 4.1 m.
