Sechelt Rapids (Skookumchuck Narrows), at the entrance to Sechelt Inlet — one of
the world's fastest tidal rapids, about 16.5 knots on the flood and 16 on the
ebb on large tides. On a 9-foot exchange an estimated 200 billion gallons pour
through a channel pinched to roughly 120 m (400 ft) by eight islets, with the
water level differing by up to about 2 m between the two sides. Transit only at
slack; prefer high-water slack, routing west of Boom Islet and giving Roland
Point a wide berth. Standing waves, whirlpools, and whitecaps build from the
current alone, even in a flat calm.

CHS publishes a primary current station for this gate — look it up under
**Sechelt Rapids**, not "Skookumchuck," in Current Tables Vol 5. Slack is brief;
the roughly 20-minute window is the practical outside bound, and the current
builds fast on either side of it.
